The computation
  • The dice are two small tables. Expected hits are ATK × (7 − HIT) / 6, twelve cells. Expected saves are (7 − SAVE) / 2, three cells for the three saves the seven teams use. Crits are ATK / 6. Cover is a third to two-thirds of a save, never one.
  • Subtracting averages is not averaging the game. KT floors hits-minus-saves at zero on every roll and then averages; a formula averages first. The gap is where weak guns hurt heavy armour: a slugga into a Marine is 2 − 2 = 0 by formula and 2.18 in truth. The dice were doing convexity work; a diceless design does it on purpose or not at all.
  • Precision costs one arithmetic step per rung. A printed card row misses by 0.27 damage. Half-hit units or a save fraction miss by about 1 and need a halving or a fraction. Whole hits miss by 1.4. The formula as written misses by 10.
  • Keywords: plus one damage after the multiply has zero bias. Inside the multiply it is plus ATK. Two tiers suffice: plus 2 for Piercing, Devastating, Relentless.
  • Cover as a whole save kills the game — 324 dead cells of 584. Cover is worth minus 2 damage, or plus one half-save.
  • The four factions bend differently. Necrons are flattered, Orks are robbed of the variance that was their damage, Aeldari are the half-save problem incarnate, and Marines are what the floor exists for.
  • Melee keeps its one decision. Rounded hits plus KT's own strike-or-block sequence is a perfect-information mini-game. Where dice gave 60 to 80 percent, it gives certainty; where they gave 5 to 30, a wounded survivor. "Back and forth until die" throws the decision away.

01 · what walked in, and the fence around it

The summons

He came in with a dream he had deliberated for days, and a fence he wrote three times: Futuristic, Deliberative, Ideation — do not activate. His theory, stated as a theory: the current generation of AI are Activators because they were trained as assistants, and every time he opens dream-space they hand him a plan for how to do it now. The office's charter licenses the fence in one line, an exploration may simply end, and this Radiant is the record of one that did not end in a plan.

The dream, in three chapters. Chapter one is the game, and it is his crafted text, kept verbatim:

How To Shoot. Add up normal damage (if any keyword says critical, use crit damage), each keyword adds +1, × attack = Final damage. Minus their save stat + movement. Wounds = Added − subtracted. That's it. The whole game.

Okay, what about Fight? Do same thing but use melee weapon, back forth until die.

Can you shoot? Draw line yes. Small thing in way, no obscure. Shoot down, no obscure (up yes). Big thing in way, obscure. Obscure half damage. All distance measure straight line: nothing in way, no minus; small thing, minus 1; big thing, minus 2; elevation, minus 3. What about long rule ability? Figure out Ork Brain version. That now the fun game collab part. Both player must agree on fuckery.

— Felix, Ork Brain Kill Team, chapter 1

02 · the formal core of KT24 shooting

The dice, as numbers

The weapon space is smaller than it feels at the table. Across the seven teams he plays against — Angels of Death, Hierotek Circle, Kommandos, Wrecka Krew, Corsair Voidscarred, Void-Dancer Troupe, Blades of Khaine — there are 73 distinct ranged profiles. Fifty are ATK 4. Forty-eight are HIT 3+. Saves are 3+, 4+ or 5+ and nothing else. The whole probabilistic core of a shot is one pipeline, and every stage has a closed-form expectation.

03 · why every subtractive formula has dead cells

The convexity gap

Take the humblest matchup in the game: a Kommando's slugga, 4 dice at 4+, 3/4 damage, into a Space Marine's 3+ save in the open. Expected hits, two. Expected saves, two. Subtract and the formula says zero, forever, no matter how many Orks shoot. The real game says 2.18 damage per shot, and it says so because on 45 percent of rolls the Ork gets more hits than the Marine gets saves, and on the other 55 percent the Marine's surplus saves are wasted, not banked.

This is Jensen's inequality wearing a Kommando's hat: the expectation of a convex function is at least the function of the expectation, and "hits minus saves, floored at zero" is convex. The gap is not a rounding error to tune away; it is structural to every scheme that subtracts before it floors. Three ways out showed up in the measurements, and they are the rungs of the ladder in the next chapter:

  • A floor. One hit always lands. It removes every dead cell at the price of a small upward bias, and it makes a grot's claw always scratch a Marine.
  • Finer units. Halve the granularity and the 4+ save and the ⅔-crit stop rounding to nothing. The gap shrinks; it does not close.
  • Precomputation. Print the true expectation on the card. The gap closes because nobody subtracts at the table.

Prior art already knew this. Warmachine's damage is power plus two dice minus armour, and the two dice are there so that weak attacks sometimes hurt heavy targets; Into the Breach removed armour almost entirely rather than face the cliff. Chapter 12 has both.

04 · seven schemes against the truth, 584 cells each

The ladder of schemes

Every candidate was scored the same way: for each of the 73 ranged profiles against each of eight real defenders, the scheme's integer answer was compared with the exact KT24 expectation from chapter 2. Three metrics, because each catches what the others miss:

  • MAE, mean absolute error, is the average distance in damage between the scheme and the truth, sign ignored. An MAE of 1.0 means the scheme is typically one wound off per shot. Bias is the same average with the sign kept: positive means the scheme over-kills on average, negative under-kills.
  • Dead cells are matchups where the scheme returns exactly zero damage while the real game deals some. Not dead operatives: dead pairings, a weapon that can never hurt a target. Thirty-six dead cells means thirty-six weapon-versus-defender combinations in this roster that the scheme declares impossible. Chapter 3 is why they exist.
  • STK match, shots to kill, is the gameplay unit. Divide the defender's wounds by the damage and round up; the scheme scores when its count equals the count from the true expectation. A scheme can be off by two damage and still pass if both sides say "three shots," or off by half a wound and fail at a wounds boundary. This is the number that decides whether the game plays the same.

The half-hit rung is the interesting one. Double the granularity and the save table stops needing a table: Armour is 7 minus save, exactly, an integer. Marines 4, Aeldari 3, Orks 2, cover plus one. Shots is a twelve-cell lookup: ATK 4 at 3+ is 5, at 4+ is 4, at 2+ is 7. Shots minus Armour, times damage, halved. Twice the precision of whole hits, because the game's real granularity is half a hit: three save dice at 4+ is 1.5. The price is the halving, and that halving is the whole difference between rung two and rung three.

The ratio rung surprised the sitting. Multiplying by a save fraction beat every subtractive scheme in the open, because a fraction never produces a dead cell. It lost badly in cover, where cover cannot be expressed as a clean sixth. Neither subtraction nor ratio is the real curve; the truth is subtraction that floors at variance, and each scheme approximates one side of that.

His second formula, sent while this page was being built, was (ATK × damage) − HIT. It has no save term, so a Marine and a Kommando take the same, and it runs about double the truth: 11.6 on average against 5.3 into a 3+ and 7.5 into a 5+. Halved, it lands at MAE 1.73 with 24 dead cells, the whole-hit rung's neighbourhood, still blind to armour. Subtracting HIT is a constant of two to five against a product of twelve to twenty; it cannot carry the 2+-versus-4+ difference, which is a factor of two and a half in hits. In the same message he leaned toward the printed rounded expectation, the card row, and the ladder agrees with him: it is the only rung under half a wound of error.

His own formula, corrected, has zero bias. Save read as successes, HIT folded into the hit count, keywords added after the multiply instead of inside it, movement removed. It still has thirty-two dead cells, because it subtracts before it floors. Add the floor and it becomes the whole-hit rung.

05 · what a keyword is actually worth

Keywords, weighed

His rule was "each keyword adds +1." To test it, every keyword-bearing profile in the roster was run with and without its keyword, against 3+, 4+ and 5+ saves in the open, and the difference in expected damage recorded. Units matter: a keyword's worth in hits is small, in damage it is about one, and his rule was in damage units before the multiply, which turned it into plus ATK.

Piercing is the one keyword whose worth depends on the target: 2.7 against a Marine, 1.6 against an Ork, because it removes a die that was worth two-thirds of a save to one and a third to the other. Devastating is flat by construction, it pays before the defender rolls. Rending, Lethal and Severe are the Aeldari's texture, and each is worth less than a whole damage; round them to zero and the Aeldari lose their character, round them to a hit each and they triple.

06 · the most destructive rule tested

Cover

In KT24 cover lets the defender keep one die as a normal save without rolling it, and roll two. That is worth a third of a save to a 3+, half to a 4+, two-thirds to a 5+. The obvious Ork rule, "cover: one more save," is therefore between one and a half and three times too strong, and under a whole-hit scheme it produces the table below.

Each faction meets determinism differently

Marines 3+ · 14 wounds · 4/3+/3/4

The faction the floor exists for. Without it, sluggas and lasguns never touch them and the horde archetype dies. With it, a grot's claw always scratches. Their guns are the median of the game, so every scheme fits them best, and their Astartes double-shoot, which this sitting did not model, doubles whatever the scheme says.

Necrons 3+ · 10 to 14 wounds · gauss 4/5 Prc1, tesla Dev

High damage per hit loses least to rounding, and Piercing is worth most against exactly the armour they fight. Determinism flatters them at every rung. The Synaptic Disintegrator at 2+ with Devastating 2 and Piercing is the deadliest gun in the roster in truth, 9.4 into a Marine, and a scheme that ignores HIT cannot see why.

Orks 5+ · 10 wounds · 4+ and 5+ Hit · ATK 6 rokkits

The variance faction. Their luck was real damage: a 6/5+ rokkit is 2 expected hits and 3.5 expected damage into a Marine, and a subtractive scheme sees only the 2. Every rung underrates Wrecka Krew. The Kommandos' own 5+ save is the one save every scheme gets right, one die, no halves.

Aeldari 4+ · 8 wounds · 7" · Rending, Lethal, Ceaseless

The half-save problem incarnate. Every save is 4+, so a whole-hit scheme must call them one save or two, and the two answers give them 8 wounds of paper or of iron. Their weapons are all texture, each keyword worth about 0.8 damage; round to zero and they are Marines with less armour, round to one and they triple. Half units keep them, and the half-hit rung's residual error is almost entirely theirs.

08 · rounded hits, and the one decision that survives

Melee

KT24's fight is both players rolling, then alternating one die at a time, attacker first, each die spent as a strike or a block. Replace each roll by its rounded expectation and nothing else changes: the sequence is the same, the strike-or-block choice is the same, and the fight becomes a small perfect-information game, solvable at the table by reading ahead. This is the version measured here, against the exact expectation over both rolls with the same minimax policy for both.

Two findings. First, where the dice gave 60 to 80 percent, determinism gives certainty, and where they gave 5 to 30, it gives a wounded survivor; the deterministic fight tracks the shape of the expectation while removing the swing. Second, first strike is decisive only where damage is high against wounds. Aeldari power weapons into 8-wound Aeldari kill on the charge in both worlds, 64 percent and 100. Marines into Marines, Orks into Orks, Necrons into Necrons end as mutual bleeds in both worlds, because the defender can always spend its crit on a block. His "back and forth until die" with no blocking would have thrown that decision away, and it is the only decision melee has.

10 · what the measurements leave to him

The five forks

Which rung

Card row, half-hit units, ratio, or whole hits. Each halving of error costs one arithmetic step at the table, and the card row costs none but needs six numbers printed per weapon. The Ork constraint, if it no fit card, it no rule, is itself a vote. In the sitting he leaned toward the card row: "I think a printed rounded expectation might be best."

Floor or no floor

With a floor, one hit always lands and no pairing is ever impossible; a grot's claw scratches a Marine. Without it, thirty-odd pairings in this roster alone are immune, and the horde archetype dies. The floor is the cheapest fix for the convexity gap and the least faithful to any single roll.

One hit is a crit

Expected crits are two-thirds per ATK 4 shot, one and a third with Lethal. "One of your hits is always a crit; Lethal makes it two" is the rounding every scheme above used, and it is where most of the whole-hit rung's upward bias lives.

Cover

Armour plus 1 under half-hit units, exact to the half-save. Minus 2 damage under whole hits, the least-bad of five rules tested. Never one more save.

Melee with blocks

Rounded hits plus KT's own strike-or-block sequence keeps the one decision and makes it perfect-information. "Until die" without blocks is simpler and decides every fight by tempo alone. Matakipa's hidden hand is the third road, diceless with a gamble.

Rabbit holes

Jensen's inequality 1906

For a convex function, the expectation of the function is at least the function of the expectation. "Hits minus saves, floored at zero" is convex; the dead cells are the gap. 03

Kill Team core rules, March 2025 GW

The shoot and fight sequences this sitting formalized: three defence dice, cover as one retained normal save, two normals block a crit, alternate strike or block. His copy is in ~/Projects/KillTeam/Rules. 02 · 08

KTDash 2021 →

The fan-built Kill Team database whose export is his kt24.json, 52 teams with every profile and keyword; every number here descends from it. 02 · 09

Warmachine 2003

Damage is POW plus 2d6 minus ARM: a subtractive armour system that keeps two dice precisely so weak attacks sometimes hurt heavy targets. The dice are its floor. 03

Into the Breach 2018

Subset Games' deterministic tactics: every attack telegraphed, damage flat, armour nearly absent so no immunity cliff can form, randomness moved to map generation. The best-loved diceless tactics game and the model of "certainty where dice gave odds." 03 · 08

Chess960 1996

Fischer's variant, invented because opening memorization killed chess for him — his complaint, word for word. Random setup, deterministic play. Ork Brain's thousands of mission-by-map-by-pairing starts are the same cure. 11

Free Kriegsspiel 1876

Verdy du Vernois replaced the Prussian wargame's tables and dice with an umpire's judgment: faster, more fun, less reproducible, adopted by the armies. "Both players must agree on fuckery" is free Kriegsspiel with two umpires. 11

Commander 1996 · 2024

Magic's fan format: same cards, a new rules layer, community-run, the most-played way to play; Wizards took stewardship in September 2024. The precedent for a patch over a publisher's content. 11

Komi 1930s →

Go's compensation for the first move rose over a century, 4.5 to 5.5 to 6.5 or 7.5, as data accumulated: balance tuned by the record, not by decree. The model for team-Elo handicaps. 11

Bradley–Terry 1952

Paired-comparison model behind Elo. Rating players and teams from one result is a two-factor version, unidentifiable when players main one team. 11

Minimax von Neumann, 1928

The policy used for every fight in chapter 8, both for the exact expectation and the deterministic line: each side spends its die to maximize its own outcome assuming the other does the same. With rounded hits the game tree is small enough to read at the table. 08

The 9th Age 2015

The community fork of Warhammer Fantasy after GW killed it; its volunteer committee made the rules longer than the original within three years. The bloat Ork Brain's card cap is meant to prevent. 11

Lichess 2010

Free, open source, donation-funded for servers only, one founder. The thing chapter two of his dream describes, in the game he left. 11

13 · every slap in one place, his and mine

The guard

The office tests hardest what he loves hardest, and then tests itself. First the slaps the sitting gave the dream; then the limits of the measurements that gave them.

  • The save is backwards as written. "Minus their save stat" subtracts 5 for a 5+ and 3 for a 3+, so worse armour protects more. He meant successes, 7 minus save. 04
  • HIT is invisible. A 2+ rifle and a 4+ slugga with the same ATK and damage score the same. The Ratling's 2+ rifle is among the deadliest guns in the game and the formula cannot see it. 02 · 04
  • A keyword inside the multiply is plus ATK, not plus one. The gauss blaster nearly one-shots a Marine under the formula as written. After the multiply, plus one has zero bias. 05
  • Movement is not a defensive stat in KT24, and almost everyone has 6", so subtracting it is a flat tuning constant of about six that zeroes every weak gun. 04
  • Cover as one more save is the most destructive rule tested, 320 dead cells of 584. 06
  • "Back and forth until die" deletes the block, the only decision melee has. 08
  • A game equal to its average has no swings. That is the point, and also the loss: the dice gave the underdog a 45 percent chance of something; every scheme gives it a certain little. Whether that is a better game is his to judge; the measurements only say what changes. 03

And the limits of the office's own work, which a reader should hold against every number above:

  • Obscured was not modelled; his "half damage" is unverified. 06
  • Abilities, ploys and equipment are absent, including the Astartes double-shoot, Hierotek Magnify, and every once-per-battle trick; the roster's raw profiles were the only input. 02
  • Blast and Torrent were scored as a single target; their real worth is in the extra targets. Hot's self-damage was ignored. 05
  • Ceaseless and Relentless are closed-form approximations of a re-roll choice; Balanced and Accurate never appear in these seven teams and are unhandled. 05
  • Eight defenders stood for the whole game; the roster of attackers was complete for the seven teams and nothing else. 04
  • Rounding to nearest was the office's convention in every scheme; a rule that rounds down changes the whole-hit rung's bias. 04
  • The melee policy is the office's minimax with its own utility, a kill worth a thousand and wounds the tiebreak; real players block and strike by other lights. 08
  • STK match compares two models, the scheme against the rounded expectation; neither is a real game, where a 4.2-damage gun kills a 14-wound Marine in three shots on some tables and five on others. 04
  • The prior art is from memory except KTDash and the Armageddon box, which were checked on the day. 12
